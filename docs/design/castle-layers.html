The Castle — five gated layers

Concentric and tall. Five nested rings that also climb, each its own district, each behind its own gate. The siege only moves inward.

Kindled · design v0.1 · 2026-08-13

Plan — nested rings

Gates are staggered, so there is no straight run to the Crown. Ring colour on each gatehouse is that front's state.

Elevation — the climb

The vertical budget is lumpy on purpose: the Ascent carries most of the height, which is why it cannot be taken by ordinary assault.

LayerGateDefensive problem — the designWhat its fall costs
L1 · The Outworks the Great Gate Frontage. More wall than bodies to hold it. The garrison visibly thins one stretch to feed another. Highest entity count in the castle — the layer that justifies the crowd tech. Mustering ground and outer wells. Reinforcement rate drops everywhere above.
L2 · The Works the Cart Gate Cover. Forge, granary, alleys, roofs. Ranged position-scores collapse; archers stop shooting and start relocating. The player watches their best asset stop working. Supply. Upkeep degrades across every remaining layer.
L3 · The Ascent the Stair Gate The ground is too good. Switchbacks, a span over a cut, murder holes. Fronts here sit at Holding almost permanently — which is exactly why the enemy takes it with a boss instead of an army. The castle's vertical advantage, in one stroke.
L4 · The Lantern Court the Lantern Gate It is full of people who are not soldiers. Healers, the wounded pulled up from below, the congregation. The root triage anchor for the whole defence lives here. Healing, army-wide. After this, attrition is one-way.
L5 · The Crown the Crown Gate Nowhere left — and that is the point. The hub: walkable, where the squad re-forms and the war is looked at from above. Visible from every layer below for the whole siege. The siege.
Front states — Holding (default: the sim stalemates by design) → Straining → Breaking → Fallen (one-way). A front does not break because more enemies arrived; it breaks because something arrived that a line of soldiers cannot answer. Your seven are the only force on the board that can be pulled off a line and sent at it.

Names are provisional. The defensive problems are not — those are the design.
