# The Pathfinder (v1 hero)
Extends: `CLASSES.md` §3 (Pathfinder — the Pack, Mark Quarry, decision hooks, C3); `WORLD.md` §2 (the Fall), §5 (the Kennel Matron's Hound), §7 (S5/S7), §8 (E5/E7). Class names are working names; per the 2026-07-11 no-names decision, every playable class is referred to by role only — no invented individual name. This file replaces the retired "Merle" fiction (kennel-lineage trapper) with a new origin per owner decision (see Canon proposals). Pronouns: they/them — deliberate, see The face.

The Pathfinder is young — no one, including them, is entirely sure how young;
records from the year the lights went out are not records, they're ash. Call it
sixteen, seventeen. They are an orphan of the Fall, what they call **the great
end war** — a child's name for a catastrophe no child could have been told the
truth of, and they have never bothered to learn the grown-up name for it. They
did not grow up in the Gatecamp. They grew up in the no-man's edge between it
and the dark: the tunnel-mouths, the collapsed districts, the dead ground where
nothing official reaches. They were not raised. They were *kept alive*, by
whatever else was out there also trying to stay that way, and some of what was
out there had four legs, and some of it had wings, and eventually the rest of it
walked up to the Gatecamp's outer patrols on two legs, filthy and armed and
entirely unbothered by the dark, leading a hawk and two dogs like it was the
most natural escort in the world.

## History

The end war did not arrive as an army. It arrived as the lights going out floor
by floor while everyone kept insisting it was temporary, and then it was not,
and then it was screaming, and then it was quiet, and the Pathfinder — small,
in someone's arms, in a corridor that does not exist anymore — was told to run
and did, and stopped being carried, and stopped being followed, and that was
the whole of the last clear memory they have of another adult who knew their
name. They do not know if that adult lived. They have stopped hoping either
answer.

What followed was years feral in the collapse-lands above and beside the
Undervault's upper works — not the deep dark, but the wreckage the Fall left on
its way down. They were not alone there, not really. A Legion signal-hawk,
unflown since her handler's post went hollow, found a child worth following
before she found one worth returning to. Two war-dogs, bred for a kennel that
no longer fed them, worked out that a starving kid with a knack for finding
grubs and blind fish was better company than starving alone. Other children
turned up the same way children do in a place like that — singly, wordless,
half-ready to bolt — and some stayed a season, and some stayed for good, and
some didn't stay anywhere ever again. The Pathfinder buried the ones they could
find enough of to bury. That is the actual center of who they are, and it
predates every bow they've ever drawn.

A Gatecamp patrol found the group — person, hawk, hounds, two other feral
kids — squatting in a collapsed relay station, fighting off a hush-maw with
thrown stone and nerve alone, and the patrol's report still reads, verbatim,
*"recommend recruitment, not rescue — they were doing fine."* They were
seventeen, maybe, by the time anyone at the Gatecamp thought to ask.

## The face

War-scarred in a way that reads before it explains: a burn or blast-scar runs
from scalp to jaw on one side, old enough to have gone smooth and pale, young
enough that the brow and cheekbone underneath sit slightly wrong — reset by
whoever had rough field skill and no time. It takes the read of the face with
it: the jawline, the brow, the places a glance uses to sort a person into a
box, are simply not there to sort. This is not androgyny and it is not a
mystery being coyly withheld — it is a specific old wound, worn without
apology, and the Gatecamp stopped asking about it before the Pathfinder was
old enough to mind. They do not correct anyone's guess. They do not confirm
one either. *"Pick whichever helps you remember to duck when I say duck."*
Hair grows in patches, cropped short everywhere to make the unevenness
uniform. Eyes never stop moving to exits and high ground — hawk-taught, or
maybe it was always going to be that way.

## The light: waylight

The Pathfinder's reserved bright is the **quarry-mark**, made from waylight —
pale lamp-lichen that grows only where honest light has burned long, cut with
tallow into a marking-paste. They did not inherit the technique. One of the
feral kids who stayed — older, a scrap of pre-Fall trail-lore still in her
head from a trade she'd never finish learning — taught it to the whole
huddled group as a way to blaze a path back to camp in the dark, the single
most useful thing anyone gave the Pathfinder for free in those years, and they
have never stopped paying it forward: every waylight mark they paint is a
promise in the same currency they were once handed. *You will not be left
wounded in the dark. Someone is coming, or something merciful is.* They know
better than most what's actually waiting for a thing left marked and
unclaimed too long — they watched it happen, more than once, before they had
a word for the Unlit or a lamp to stop it. The mark is what they have instead
of a lamp.

## Voice

Clipped. Words are for when pointing doesn't work, and they were raised by
things that don't talk at all.

1. *(marking)* "Marked. It ends clean, or it ends spared. Not left."
2. *(loosing the hawk)* "Go look. Come back. I mean both halves."
3. *(bonding a stray)* "You too, huh. Yeah. Me too. Stay near the fire, I don't share it with much."
4. *(a pack death)* "…Found you again. Wish it'd been sooner." *(a beat)* "Dig's this way."

## The Pack

Every member named — the one indulgence the Pathfinder allows themself, because
in the wreckage-years a name was the only thing that could be given away for
free and still mean something. The hawk is **Relay**, her old Legion
designation kept as a name on purpose: nobody ever called her anything before
the Pathfinder did, and taking a service-tag and making it a person's name is
the whole of their politics in one word. The hounds are **Latch** and **Ash** —
not royal stock, just survivors, thick-coated mongrel descendants of Legion
war-dogs gone feral generations back. Scouts are the human half of the Pack:
other kids the collapse-lands produced, grown now, some of whom the Pathfinder
found and some of whom found *them* — the roles blur, deliberately, because
that's the truth of how the group actually formed. The Pathfinder trains new
scouts the only way they know: by surviving next to them until it takes.

The Pack is small because a name is a debt, and the Pathfinder will not carry
more debts than they can pay. When a member falls and is found again, changed
(C3), the hunt-law holds without needing to be said aloud: theirs is a marked
quarry now, and they go looking, and nobody else is permitted to loose at it
first.

## Decision hooks

- **E5 · The Rescue Site (Pathfinder-flavored):** the kennels and menageries.
  Penned animals bolt at a loud breach — into the dark, into the fight, into
  a Legion pike-rank; the quiet route risks the weakest of them fading before
  the locks give. In co-op the site splits naturally: the Vanguard takes the
  people, the Pathfinder takes the kennels, and something about a caged animal
  makes them noticeably less clipped for the length of the fight.
- **Class hook — the other stray:** a rescue site turns up a feral child,
  alone, unclaimed, doing exactly what the Pathfinder was doing at that age.
  Bond them into the Pack — a fast elite recruit, and one more name on a
  wrist that already has too many — or hand them up to the Gatecamp to be
  raised properly, ordinary, a life the Pathfinder never got. They have never
  once chosen the Pack. They have never once stopped hesitating first.
- **Class hook — the dire beast:** the thing that killed a Pack member can be
  spared at the kill and bonded instead. A marked quarry spared is spared *on
  purpose*, forever — so taking in the monster that took from them isn't
  revenge and isn't weakness, it's the hardest possible keeping of their own
  law. The grave gets dug either way.
- **E7 · The Dark Bargain (private):** the whisper offers the end of losing
  anyone — *a Pack that cannot die, strays that always come home, no more
  graves to dig.* It does not mention that "found again, changed" (C3) is
  already the dark's version of that exact bargain, paid out in installments,
  at a price it would prefer they never compare against the offer in full.
- **N4 · the Kennel Matron's Hound:** the feral pack-mother of the old royal
  kennels does not answer to any whistle-code — the Pathfinder never learned
  one. She answers the way one thing that kept its litter alive through the
  end war recognizes another. Bonding her is the Pathfinder's apex hook;
  freeing her litter to the Gatecamp is the closest they come to giving
  someone else the childhood they didn't get.

## At the Gatecamp

Sleeps in the kennel-row, or on the wall-top when the camp is too loud, which
is usually. Runs tunnel-games for the camp's children and insists, unconvincingly,
that it isn't kindness. If the kennels are freed (S5), the camp's hounds orbit
them like a second, off-duty Pack, and they perform great annoyance about it.
They stand at the back of gatherings, count the exits, and leave before the
singing — but the cord of tokens on their wrist is camp-made now: someone
braids the cord-stock and leaves it at the kennel-row without a word, and the
Pathfinder has never once asked who, which, for them, is as close to trust as
it gets.
