# The Relickeeper (v1 hero)
Extends: `CLASSES.md` §2 (Relickeeper — the Graver, the Awakened, decision hooks); `WORLD.md` §5 (the Doorwarden), §8 (E5/E7/E8); `docs/art/aesthetic-direction.md` §1g (ember vs. cold-geometry magic temperatures). Class names are working names; per the 2026-07-11 no-names decision, every playable class is referred to by role only — no invented individual name (see Canon proposals).

The Relickeeper is sixty-four, the last apprentice of the Vault-Tenders,
the hereditary guild that kept the kingdom's wards fed and its guardians on shift.
Not a wizard: a maintainer. The kingdom called her family janitors and her family
agreed, proudly. She was an apprentice when the lights went out, and the guild died
on the lower floors trying to keep them lit. Everything she does in the Undervault
she describes the same way: *catching up on the backlog.*

## History

The Vault-Tenders held the maintenance rites — how to re-ink a ward-line before it
starved, how to mend a sentinel without waking it wrong, which guardians answered
to which duty-posts. She learned them at nine, holding the lamp for her mother.
When the fall came, the guild went down to their posts and did not come up, and a
girl with half an apprenticeship became the only living person who knows how the
kingdom's immune system works. She spent decades keeping the Gatecamp's hinges and
seals alive, telling herself that counted. Then the runs began, and she went down
with the second one, and found a sentinel still standing its post in the dark,
cracked through and waiting for relief that never came. She has not stopped
descending since. To her the Undervault is not a tomb and not a dungeon. It is a
neglected building, and she has the keys, and broken means fixable.

## The face

Small, straight-backed, mason's forearms; white hair cropped practical under a
tender's cap, stone-dust permanently in the creases of her knuckles and the lines
of her face. Round work-spectacles — one lens is a decades-old replacement she
ground herself from ward-glass, very slightly warm in the right light, which she
denies. The Graver rides her back on a strap like a mason's tool, because it is
one. Expression: dry, appraising, unhurried — the look of a professional estimating
a job everyone else calls impossible.

## The light: the waking-ink

The Relickeeper's reserved bright is the **rune-ember** — and hers is waking-ink,
the Vault-Tenders' own pigment: ember-ash bound in wax, the stuff her guild used to
feed ward-lines for a thousand years. She carries her mother's last jar, and renders
a little more from the ash of every shrine she restores — the ink literally comes
back as the kingdom does. Every mark the Graver leaves glows with it: warm, an ember
under ash, the temperature of awakening. She is precise about the distinction: the
Crown's sigils are cold geometry, hers are banked fires. *"Their marks keep things
still. Mine wake things up. Do not confuse the two in my hearing."*

## Voice

Workplace dryness, procedure as long habit. She talks to the guardians like
coworkers, because that is what they are.

1. *(mending a sentinel)* "There you are. Your relief is late — decades late. I'm her. Complaint noted. Hold still."
2. *(awakening)* "Post is the east arch, same as it ever was. The kingdom apologizes for the interruption."
3. *(Graver resonance)* "Something under this floor is still on shift. Let's go clock it in."
4. *(a cracked guardian holding)* "Cracks are honest work. It's the quiet stones you check twice."

## The Awakened

Staff, not constructs — she will correct you. She knows their duty-names and their
posts: the arch-wardens, the processional plinths, the reliquary that used to lead
festival marches. Over-mending a guardian to its gilded state is her one
indulgence, and she performs it like a foreman granting a bonus. When one cracks,
she scolds it fondly and books the repair; when one is lost past mending, she takes
its duty-name off the roster herself, with the Graver, into stone — the Tenders'
way of retiring a post with honor. She restores rather than plunders by conviction
so old it's structural: relics are not loot, they are *deferred maintenance*.

## Decision hooks

- **E5 · The Rescue Site (Relickeeper-flavored):** the Legion uses captive
  guardians as gate-weights and haulage — administration finds a use for
  everything. A loud breach risks cracking what she came to free past mending; the
  quiet route means long minutes of careful unchaining while the party holds its
  breath. Her yield is the party's future fortress; her haste is its ruin.
- **Class hook — the dark-fused guardian:** awaken it anyway (a strong hybrid, and
  a colleague back on shift) or purify it (weaker, but the shrine stays cleansed)?
  Her professional horror is not darkness — it is *waste*. Purifying means
  accepting a loss she could have inventoried as a save.
- **Class hook — relic fragments:** spend now or carry toward the set-piece
  guardian, lost on death. She carries. She always carries. It has killed her
  before.
- **E7 · The Dark Bargain (private):** the whisper speaks her language, and its
  pitch is *perfect preservation*: nothing decays in the quiet, nothing crumbles,
  nothing is ever lost again — a kingdom held forever exactly as it was. It is a
  conservator's heaven and it is the Crown's whole project, and the whisper knows
  she knows the difference, and offers anyway.
- **E8 / N5 · The Doorwarden:** the multi-run fragment chain is her arc — the
  biggest job on the guild's books, the one her mother's generation never finished.

## At the Gatecamp

A workbench near the forge-site (livelier once N2 opens — she and Maro argue
taxonomy across the aisle: he calls a fragment scrap, she calls his hammer scrap,
honors even). She maintains the camp's gate-seals and hinges without being asked
and keeps a cloth-covered board of relic fragments laid out like a surgery.
Children are permitted to look and not touch, and she narrates the fragments'
duty-posts to them, which is the closest thing the camp has to a history lesson.
