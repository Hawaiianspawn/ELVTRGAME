# The Vanguard (v1 hero)
Extends: `CLASSES.md` §1 (Vanguard — the Liberated, Banner Slam, decision hooks); `WORLD.md` §5 (Maro the Chainwright), §8 (E4/E5/E7); `docs/art/aesthetic-direction.md` §4 decision 5 (two-register record). Class names are working names; per the 2026-07-11 no-names decision, every playable class is referred to by role only — no invented individual name (see Canon proposals).

The Vanguard is thirty-eight, a quarryman's son from the Gatecamp who spent nine
years in a Legion pen and came out of it certain of exactly one thing: *nobody is
a number.* The Legion's ledger listed him as a row and a mark. He answers with
names now — his own, his soldiers', and the name of every person he has ever
walked out of the dark, stitched onto the banner he fights under.

## History

He went down young, on one of the camp's early salvage crews, and the crew was taken
on the second floor: ledgered, marched in a prisoner column, penned. Two rescues
touched his pen in nine years. The first party took the quiet route — slow, careful,
and too late for half of them. The second broke the doors loud; the alarm brought
the whole floor's muster down, and the woman who led the breach died holding the
stair so the freed could climb it. He has stood on both sides of E5's arithmetic,
and he does not pretend either answer is clean.

He was carrying a shield within a year of coming up. He has led descents ever since —
not because he loves fighting, but because he keeps the count, and the count of
people still in the pens is not zero.

## The face

Big, heavy-boned, deliberate. Broken nose set crooked; short dark beard going grey
early at the jaw; a ring of old chain-gall scarring at the throat where the collar
sat for nine years — visible above the gorget, always. His helm is Legion-pattern
with the visor unbolted and gone: an open face, on principle. *"A banner needs a
face under it. They followed enough closed helms."* Eyes tired and kind and always
counting.

## The light: the Roll

The Vanguard's reserved bright is the **banner** — and his banner is the Roll:
a Legion muster-flag turned inside out, scoured of the crown, restitched by his own
hands. Every person he has brought out alive is stitched onto the field in
lamp-waxed thread, one counted name per rescue, in the sampler-stitch of the
kingdom's record — *to be stitched in is to be witnessed*, and the people the ledger
reduced to marks go onto the Roll as names. Along the hem, folded under where only
he handles it, are the names of those he didn't bring out. The first name on the hem
is the woman who died on the stair for him. When he plants the banner and the
Liberated fight to the death beneath it, they are fighting under their own names.

## Voice

Full sentences, unhurried, the logistics of care. He counts out loud the way other
men breathe.

1. "Count off. …Good. Same number as the last door. We keep it that way."
2. "You're not a prisoner as of this door. You're rear rank, third file. It's better work."
3. *(Veteran promotion)* "Two floors and still standing. Give me your name — it goes on the cloth when we're out."
4. *(The Muster, ult)* "The Legion calls this a muster. So do I. Mine answers."

## The Liberated

He will not say "units." He says *the third file*, *the two from the flooded pen*,
*Osk's rank*. Every head-count is a promise renewed; a formation to him is not a
tactic, it's the shape of people keeping each other alive. Veterans are the ones
whose names he has had time to learn — a Bannerman's mini-banner is a strip cut
from the Roll itself, carried by someone whose name is on it. His grief runs
quiet and administrative in the best sense: when one falls, the line holds first,
and the name is said right at the next quiet, and then it goes on the hem.

## Decision hooks

- **E5 · The Rescue Site (Vanguard-flavored):** loud breach or quiet route — he has
  survived one and buried the other. Whichever the party picks, he is the one
  who can say, from the inside, what it will cost. Event text should let him.
- **Class hook — prison block vs. armory:** the armory is the choice that left his
  pen waiting, once. He will run that math again from the other side of the door,
  and hate it, and run it honestly.
- **Class hook — a fallen ally's veterans:** fold them into his line, or let them
  carry their own banner as an honor guard? His instinct is the honor guard —
  names belong to their own cloth — but a line that's thinning tempts.
- **E4 · The Prisoner Column:** he was marched in one. Shadowing a column to the
  pens instead of striking now is the hardest ask the game can make of him.
- **E7 · The Dark Bargain (private):** the whisper offers him the Legion's own
  gift — *a line that cannot break, ranks that never fall, no more names for the
  hem.* Order without loss. It is precisely what he wants, and it is precisely
  what the Still Legion is.

## At the Gatecamp

He keeps the muster board by the gate: who's down, who's due back, who's new. The
Roll hangs above it between runs, and rescued people find their names on it —
some come every day at first, just to look. At dusk he head-counts the camp,
which the camp pretends not to notice, and would miss terribly. If Maro's forge is
open (N2), the two men have an old, wordless account between them: some of the
shackle-scars on his wrists are Maro's own work, from the chains the Legion
made him forge — and both of them know it, and Maro has never once been asked to
apologize, and quietly never stops.
